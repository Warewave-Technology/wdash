Three marks for WDash

The navbar currently carries a Font Awesome magnifying glass. It is an icon the product borrowed, it means search on every other site that uses it, and it says nothing about the two signals WDash has that a search box does not. These three are drawn for it. Nothing is wired in — pick one first.

Every mark is one file. Ink follows the page; one element takes the accent.

For
The brackets are the product's own idiom, and the line is the only thing on the screen that moves. It crosses them on purpose: what is being watched does not stop at the edge of the window.
Against
It reads "monitoring" before it reads "WDash", and every uptime tool on the market owns a heartbeat.

For
One wide pane with a line in it and two narrow ones beside — the layout every screen in this product already has. Four rectangles and a stroke, so it is the most legible of the three at 16 pixels.
Against
The most literal and the least distinctive: a rounded rectangle holding a sparkline is a shape a dozen tools already use.

One file each, no light and dark variants to keep in step. Everything except one element is currentColor, so a mark takes the ink of whatever it sits in; the pulse, the live point and the sparkline are var(--wdash-accent), which the palette sets. Press One colour above to see what survives when the accent is taken away — a mark that needs two colours to work is a mark that breaks in a favicon.

The files are in docs/logo/, beside the sheet they were checked against.
